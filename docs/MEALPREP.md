# Halal meal-prep library: breakfast, lunch and dinner

**Owner:** Yusuf Benali (Nutrition Lab) · **Reviewed by:** Dr. Ayesha Siddiqui (lunch boxes reviewed in Sprint 1; breakfast, dinner and no-cook preps pending) · **Prices and labels read:** 2026-10-13 (lunch), 2026-10-14 (breakfast and dinner additions, no-cook preps, ready picks), 2026-10-15 (the two weeks), 2026-10-16 (dal and bulgur prices)

64 halal preps in three tiers. **43 to batch-cook** on Sunday: **8 breakfasts**, **22 lunch boxes** to carry to university and **13 freezer dinners** to eat at home. **21 no-cook preps** you assemble in 15 minutes or less from market food. And a list of **ready-to-eat picks** that need nothing at all. In the app, search for the prep and log **"1 jar"**, **"1 box"** or **"1 portion"** (or the ½ serving). The Prep library filter *All · Cook · No-cook* uses the same tiers.

Every number comes from a standard recipe calculation (NUTRITION.md §6). Each recipe sheet shows the ingredients in grams, the published recipes it starts from, the yields, storage per component, a shopping list and a dated cost.

**How to make it:** every recipe sheets has a step-by-step method for a beginner (equipment, what to cut first, each step with its heat and time, how to tell it is done, how to box it, how to reheat). The app shows the same method under "How to make it" on the recipe card.

## No-cook preps

Each one meets the same targets per meal as the cooked preps (`RULES.mealPrep.targetsBySlot`): breakfast 300–550 kcal and 25–40 g protein, lunch 550–750 kcal and 35–50 g, dinner 500–800 kcal and 30–50 g, snacks 150–300 kcal and 15–30 g, with the fibre minimums. - **The lunch-box test.** Every lunch here travels: a closed box, nothing that leaks, good cold or with one item (a sealed rice cup or the box itself) in the microwave. Fish and eggs are never microwaved; sauces and dips go in a small pot; pitas are filled at lunch so they stay dry.
- **Sealed packs.** Microwave rice cups, cans and single Burgos tubs travel sealed inside the box. They keep to the date on the pack, so a box can be packed 2 days ahead without breaking the 1-day rule for cooked rice. Soaked couscous is cooked starch: that box is made the night before.
- **Eggs.** Boil 6–10 on Sunday or Tuesday night (10 minutes) and keep them in their shells: 3 days in the fridge. Most of these preps use them.
- **Feta is "check".** The feta at Mercadona lists *cuajo* (rennet) without saying its origin. The lentil salad has a halal twin: #40 is the same salad with a sealed tub of queso de Burgos 0% (*cuajo microbiano*) instead of feta, as its own record, and #29 carries `halalSwap` → #40 in the app data, which the app uses for its "Halal swap" button on the feta box. #29 (feta) stays for people who accept it.
- **Bread.** Mercadona's own barras list E-472e with no origin (product pages read 2026-10-14). For pa amb tomàquet buy a barra at a *forn* made with flour, water, salt and yeast, or ask. The Mission pita (no E471) and Hacendado picos integrales (no lard) are fine.

## Roti and paratha stock

The dinners (and two breakfasts) use a **weekly roti stock** made in one go. Each medium roti is 40 g cooked, from about 29 g wholemeal atta and 21 g water, and is 98 kcal (Zainab's `pk-roti` record: 246 kcal, 7.8 g protein and 8.3 g fibre per 100 g).

- **Batch:** 500 g atta + 350 g water + 2–3 g salt makes about 17 medium roti (the same dough as the recipe sheets: yield 0.799 after cooking).
- Knead, rest 20 min, divide into 50 g balls, roll thin and cook on a dry tawa 30–40 s a side.
- Cool under a cloth, then freeze in stacks of 2 or 4 between sheets of baking paper.
- Reheat from frozen on a hot tawa, 20–30 s a side, or 15–20 s in the microwave under a damp paper towel.
- Extra roti you eat on their own: log them as **Roti / chapati (home, no ghee)**, "1 roti" (40 g, 98 kcal). Parathas: the breakfast aloo paratha and anda-paratha roll sheets give a lighter paratha with 6 g oil each, frozen the same way.

- **Use-by** of a jar, box or portion = the day it was cooked + the shortest fridge life of its parts. A frozen one has no use-by until you move it to the fridge; then it keeps 1 day.
- **Rice, pasta, couscous and bulgur keep 1 day** in the fridge: in a rice batch, box 1 goes in the fridge and the rest in the freezer. Move tomorrow's box down the night before.
- **Cool fast:** spread food on a tray and get it in the fridge within an hour.
- **Reheat once,** until steaming hot right through (75 °C). Raita, yogurt and salad pots are never microwaved.
- **Cold lunch boxes** travel in an insulated bag with an ice pack; eat within 2 hours of leaving the fridge (1 hour on a day above 32 °C).

## A Sunday that works

About 2½ hours for a week of breakfasts, lunches and dinners, cooked in this order so the oven and the hob are both busy:

1. **Roti stock first** (dough rests while you chop).
2. **One dinner** on the hob (karahi, keema, aloo gosht or daal), 4 portions.
3. **One lunch box** batch, 4 boxes: rice, couscous or pasta last, because they keep 1 day in the fridge.
4. **One oven breakfast** while the hob works: egg muffins at 180 °C, or pancakes on a second pan.
5. **Overnight oats** last, in 5 minutes, straight into the fridge.
6. Cool everything on trays, pack, label with the date, and put tomorrow's things in the fridge and the rest in the freezer.

Pick a different lunch and dinner each week, one hot and one cold lunch, so Thursday is not the same as Monday. Over a month the freezer builds up a mix of dinners.

## How to batch in the app

Everything happens in the **Prep** tab.

1. **Cook a batch.** Under *Library*, tap a prep (grouped by breakfast, lunch and dinner), set the day you cooked it and how many jars, boxes or portions it made (standard: 3 or 4). The app puts the first ones in the fridge, as many as the fridge life allows, and the rest in the freezer; change any of them.
2. **Your boxes.** The batch card lists each box with its use-by: "last day" on the day, "past use-by, best not to eat" after it. Moving a frozen box to the fridge starts its 1-day clock.
3. **Place into a slot.** Pick the meal (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner…) and tap the days you will eat it, one tap per day. The day's totals count it at once; ticking it eaten uses the box, deleting the entry frees it.
4. **Shopping list.** On the batch card, *Shopping list* → *Copy list*: halal butcher first, then the supermarket. Every ingredient is a food in the database except the ground spices and salt, which appear by name.

Your own pots work the same way: *New recipe* in the Prep tab, then cook a batch of it.

The nutrition of a library prep always comes from its Lab record (this page), never from adding up the shopping list.

## Halal shopping in Barcelona

**Meat:** buy chicken and beef from a halal butcher (*carnicería halal*): there are several in the Raval, Sant Antoni and Poble Sec, and online halal butchers deliver in the province. Supermarket chicken and *caldo de pollo* (stock) are not halal unless the pack carries a halal certification seal.

**Labels:** read the ingredients, not the front of the pack.

- *Manteca* or *manteca de cerdo* is lard. *Mantequilla* is butter (fine). *Manteca de cacao* is cocoa butter (fine).
- *Gelatina* is usually pork gelatine unless it says *bovina halal* or *vegetal*. Check yogurts, desserts, sweets and "light" dairy.
- *E471* and *E472* (mono- and diglycerides) can come from animal fat. Accept them only when the label says *de origen vegetal*. On 2026-10-13 every wheat wrap at Mercadona Barcelona listed E471 with no origin, which is why the shami box uses Mission pita (no E471) and the breakfast wrap uses a home-made roti. Hacendado hummus marks its additives as *de origen vegetal*.
- *Sebo* is tallow. *Jamón, chorizo, fuet, sobrasada, panceta, butifarra* are pork. *Sin cerdo* ("no pork") is not the same as halal.
- Wine hides in sauces, stocks and stews (*al vino*, *con vino blanco*, *jerez*, *coñac*, *vi ranci*). These recipes use lemon or water instead.
- Protein powder: whey comes from cheese-making, and not every brand is halal-certified. None of these preps needs it.
- Cheese: some people avoid cheese made with animal rennet (*cuajo animal*). Labels that say *cuajo microbiano* or *coagulante vegetal* avoid the question. Mercadona's queso de Burgos 0% says *cuajo microbiano* and its queso fresco batido has no rennet at all (both halal); its feta does not say (check), so #29 is marked check and #40 is its Burgos twin.

**Bakeries and cafés:** a croissant *de mantequilla* is made with butter. Cheap pastries and some *bollería* are made with lard, and an *ensaïmada* is made with pork lard (*saïm*) by definition. When in doubt, ask "¿lleva manteca de cerdo?" (Catalan: "porta saïm?"). *Pa amb tomàquet* and *café con leche* are fine; most bocadillos are pork, so pick *tortilla* or *atún*.

**Desi grocery:** masoor and chana dal, bulgur, green chillies, whole spices, garam and chaat masala are on the Pakistani or Moroccan shelf at or next to the halal butcher (Raval). Mercadona Barcelona sold none of masoor dal, chana dal or bulgur on the dates checked.

**Cross-contact is not tracked.** Katori marks each food halal, check or not halal from its ingredients. It does not track shared slicers, grills, fryers or bread boards in cafés, bakeries and butchers. If that matters to you, ask.

## Where the numbers come from

- Ingredient values: USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy and Foundation), IFCT 2017 (chana dal, wholemeal atta), and product labels read on 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14 (Open Food Facts entries checked against the Mercadona product page).
- Cooking yields: Bognár A. (2002), *Tables on weight yield of food and retention factors of food constituents*; water balances from USDA raw and cooked pairs; for roti and parathas, Zainab Rauf's moisture-based yields (Rasool et al. 2006, FNDDS, CoFID 2021).

- Prices: tienda.mercadona.es (Barcelona, postal code 08001) read 2026-10-13, 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15 (the two weeks); carnehalalonline.com read 2026-10-13; www.indianflavours.es (dals) read 2026-10-16 and elturco.es (bulgur) read 2026-10-16. Prices change: the date is part of the number.
