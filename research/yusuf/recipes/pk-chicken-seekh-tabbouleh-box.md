## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Big mixing bowl
- Grater
- Clean tea towel or kitchen paper for squeezing
- Oven tray lined with baking paper (skewers optional)
- Heatproof bowl with a plate as a lid
- Kettle
- Sharp knife and chopping board
- Wire rack or plate for cooling
- 4 lunch boxes and 3 freezer bags

**Before you start**

- Grate about 1½ medium onions (150 g), then squeeze the grated onion hard in a tea towel to get the juice out. Wet onion makes kebabs that fall apart.
- Peel and grate 3 garlic cloves (10 g) and a 3 cm piece of ginger (10 g) into a paste.
- Chop 2 green chillies (10 g), a handful of coriander (15 g) and a few mint leaves (5 g) very fine.

**Steps**

1. In the bowl mix the chicken mince (640 g) with the squeezed onion, ginger-garlic paste, chillies, coriander and mint, and the powders: 2 tsp roasted ground cumin (4 g), 2 tsp garam masala (4 g), ¾ tsp red chilli powder (2 g), ¾ tsp chaat masala (2 g), ½ tsp black pepper (1 g) and 1 tsp salt (6 g). Knead with your hand until sticky. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Cover and chill 30 minutes: it makes the kebabs firmer. *(no heat · 30 min)*
3. Heat the oven to 220 °C with the grill on (or fan). Brush the lined tray with half the oil (5 g, 1 tsp). *(oven 220 °C · 10 min)*
4. With wet hands shape 16 sausages of about 50 g (12 cm long, 2.5 cm thick) on the tray, or press them around flat skewers. Brush them with the other 1 tsp oil (5 g). *(no heat · 8 min)*
5. Bake 8 minutes, turn them, then 7–10 minutes more until browned at the edges. *(oven 220 °C · 17 min)*
6. Check: break the thickest kebab open. White all through, no pink, clear juices, browned at the edges. Pink? 3–4 minutes more in the oven. *(no heat)*
7. Let the kebabs cool on the rack. *(no heat · 20 min)*
8. Bulgur (no cooking on the hob): pour 300 ml boiling water (about 1¼ cups) from the kettle over the bulgur (200 g, about 1 cup) in the heatproof bowl. Stir, cover with the plate and leave 20 minutes until the water is soaked up. Fluff with a fork and spread it out to cool. *(no heat · 20 min)*
9. Split the cooled bulgur into 4 portions (about 125 g each) and the kebabs into 4 sets of 4. *(no heat)*
10. Tabbouleh, first half (no cooking): wash and dry the parsley (60 g, about 1½ bunches) and mint (10 g); pick the leaves off the thick stalks and chop them very fine. Dice 150 g tomato, 100 g cucumber (5 mm) and 20 g red onion (very fine). *(no heat · 10 min)*
11. Whisk 2 tbsp lemon juice (30 g), 1½ tbsp extra virgin olive oil (20 g) and a heaped ¼ tsp salt (2 g). Toss with the chopped herbs and vegetables. Fold Monday's bulgur portion into half of this salad; keep the other half in a box for Tuesday. *(no heat · 3 min)*
12. Fill tomorrow's box: ¼ of the tabbouleh with its bulgur and 4 kebabs on the side. Put each other set of 4 kebabs with a bulgur portion in a freezer bag, pressed flat. Make the second half of the salad the same way on Tuesday night. *(no heat)*

**Done when:** Break the thickest kebab open: white all through, no pink, juices clear (75 °C if you have a thermometer); browned at the edges.

**Box it up:** Cool and put away within 1 hour. Soaked bulgur counts as cooked grain (1 day in the fridge), so it freezes with its kebabs. Box 1 in the fridge: eat it the next day. Boxes 2–4 in the freezer: eat within 1 month, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. Mix the bulgur into the salad in the morning. The dressed salad keeps 2 days and does not freeze: half on Sunday, half on Tuesday night.

**Reheat:** Made to eat cold: carry it in an insulated bag with an ice pack and eat within 2 hours of leaving the fridge. To eat the kebabs warm, microwave them alone, covered, 60–90 seconds until hot all through. Never microwave the tabbouleh.

**Tips**

- Halal: chicken mince from the halal butcher. Ask them to mince some thigh with the breast: it stays juicier.
- Kebabs falling apart? Squeeze the onion harder, chill the mix longer, and shape them flat on the tray instead of on skewers.
- Bulgur is at Turkish and Pakistani grocers ("bulgur" or "dalia"); fine or medium both work. Flat-leaf parsley is perejil at Mercadona.
- Chaat masala (Pakistani grocery) gives the tang. No chaat masala? Use a squeeze of lemon and a pinch of salt.
