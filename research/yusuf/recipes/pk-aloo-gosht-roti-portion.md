## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Heavy pot or casserole (3–4 l) with a lid (or a pressure cooker)
- Wooden spoon
- Grater
- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop for rolling
- Tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan (26–28 cm)
- Clean tea towel
- Wide tray for cooling
- 4 freezer boxes

**Before you start**

- Cut the beef (600 g, boneless) into 3 cm cubes if the butcher has not.
- Slice about 2 medium onions (200 g) thin. Chop 2 medium tomatoes (240 g).
- Peel 5 garlic cloves (15 g) and a 4 cm piece of ginger (15 g); grate both into a paste.
- Peel about 3 medium potatoes (600 g) and cut them into 8 big chunks (about 75 g each). Keep them in a bowl of cold water.
- Whisk the yogurt (60 g, 4 tbsp) smooth. Chop a small handful of coriander (8 g).

**Steps**

1. Roti dough first, so it rests while you cook: in a big bowl mix 235 g atta (wholemeal chapati flour, about 2 cups) with a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour in 165 ml water (a little under ¾ cup) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until it comes together in one soft, slightly sticky ball. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Knead 5 minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, repeat. Wet your hand if it sticks. It is ready when it is smooth and soft, like an earlobe. *(no heat · 5 min)*
3. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and leave the dough to rest while you cook (at least 20 minutes: a rested dough rolls thin without tearing). *(no heat)*
4. Heat 2½ tbsp sunflower oil (36 g) in the pot for 1 minute. Add the onion and stir every minute or two until golden brown (not dark). *(medium heat · 10 min)*
5. Add the ginger-garlic paste and the beef. Turn the heat up and stir until the meat is browned on the outside. *(high heat · 5 min)*
6. Add the tomatoes and the whisked yogurt, then 1¼ tsp salt (8 g), 1½ tsp red chilli powder (4 g), 2 tsp ground coriander (4 g), a heaped ½ tsp haldi (turmeric powder, 2 g) and 1 tsp ground cumin (2 g). *(medium heat)*
7. Stir and cook until the tomato has melted into a thick paste and the oil separates at the edges ("bhunai"). If it sticks, add 2 tbsp of the measured water. *(medium heat · 9 min)*
8. Add 400 ml water. Bring it to the boil, put the lid on and simmer gently until a piece of beef breaks easily with a spoon (pressure cooker: 25 minutes after it whistles). Check every 20 minutes and add a splash of water if it gets dry. *(low heat · 70 min)*
9. Divide the dough into 8 equal balls of about 50 g (golf-ball size) and keep them under the cloth. Heat the tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan, with no oil, for 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
10. Roll one ball: dip it in dry atta, flatten it with your palm and roll it into a thin circle the size of a side plate (about 16 cm across, 2 mm thick). Give it a quarter turn every few rolls so it stays round, and dust with atta when it sticks. *(no heat · 1 min)*
11. First side: lay the roti on the hot dry pan. When small bubbles show (20–30 seconds), flip it. *(medium-high heat · 30 s)*
12. Second side: cook 30–40 seconds, pressing the edges gently with a folded cloth so it puffs. Flip once more for 10 seconds, then lay it on a clean tea towel. *(medium-high heat · 1 min)*
13. Roll and cook the other 7 the same way, rolling the next one while one cooks. On a first try each one takes 2–3 minutes, so allow about 18 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 18 min)*
14. Let the roti cool fully, spread out on the tea towel (about 10 minutes), before you bag them: bagged warm, they go soggy. *(no heat · 10 min)*
15. Drain the potatoes and add them with another 200 ml water. Lid half on, simmer until a knife slides into the potatoes easily and the gravy (shorba) is light and runny. *(low heat · 22 min)*
16. Turn off the heat. Stir in 1 tsp garam masala (2 g) and the coriander. *(no heat)*
17. Check: a piece of beef breaks apart easily with a spoon and a knife slides into the potatoes. Firm? Lid on, 10 minutes more. *(no heat)*
18. Spread the aloo gosht on the tray to cool and set a timer for 30 minutes. When it rings, put the tray in the fridge, even if the roti are not finished: cooked food never sits out more than 1 hour. *(no heat)*
19. Pack 4 portions: 2 potato chunks, ¼ of the cold beef and shorba in each box, and 2 cooled roti in a small bag. *(no heat)*

**Done when:** The beef breaks apart easily with a spoon, the potatoes are soft right through, and the shorba is thin. Roti: light brown spots on both sides, no pale or doughy patches, soft when folded.

**Box it up:** Cool on a wide tray and put away within 1 hour. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. Each portion goes with its 2 roti. Potatoes go a little softer after freezing; that is normal.

**Reheat:** Move a frozen portion to the fridge the night before. Microwave covered 3–4 minutes, stirring halfway, until steaming hot all through (from frozen: defrost setting first, then 4 minutes). Add a splash of water if the shorba has thickened. Reheat once only. Roti: 20–30 seconds a side on a hot dry pan (from frozen, 30–40 seconds a side), or 15 seconds in the microwave under a damp paper towel.

**Tips**

- Halal: buy the beef from the halal butcher (carnicería halal). Check the yogurt label says only milk and cultures (no gelatine).
- Ask for stewing beef ("ternera para guisar", for example morcillo or aguja). Lean steak goes dry and tough here.
- This takes about 2 hours, mostly simmering: start it on a Sunday afternoon, or use a pressure cooker.
- Beef still tough? It needs more time, not more heat: 15 minutes more with the lid on.
- Too spicy? Use 1 tsp chilli powder instead of 1½.
- Short on time? Buy fresh or frozen wholemeal roti (chapati) at the Pakistani grocery near the halal butcher and warm each on a dry pan 30 seconds a side. Shop roti are often bigger and some have oil in them, so the numbers in the app change a little.
