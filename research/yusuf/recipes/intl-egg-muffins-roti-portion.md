## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- 12-hole muffin tin (or 12 silicone muffin moulds)
- Pastry brush or kitchen paper
- Jug or bowl and a whisk or fork
- Wire rack
- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop for rolling
- Tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan (26–28 cm)
- Clean tea towel
- Baking paper
- Freezer bags and 1–2 boxes for the fridge

**Before you start**

- Chop the baby spinach (120 g). Dice about 1½ red peppers (160 g) small (5 mm). Chop the red onion fine (80 g, ½ medium), 2 green chillies (10 g) and a small handful of coriander (8 g).
- Take the frozen peas (120 g, about 1 cup) out of the bag; they go in frozen.

**Steps**

1. Roti dough first, so it rests while you cook: in a big bowl mix 117 g atta (wholemeal chapati flour, about 1 cup) with a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour in 82 ml water (about ⅓ cup) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until it comes together in one soft, slightly sticky ball. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Knead 5 minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, repeat. Wet your hand if it sticks. It is ready when it is smooth and soft, like an earlobe. *(no heat · 5 min)*
3. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and leave the dough to rest while you cook (at least 20 minutes: a rested dough rolls thin without tearing). *(no heat)*
4. Heat the oven to 180 °C. Brush the 12 holes of the tin with 2 tsp oil (8 g), right up the sides. *(oven 180 °C · 10 min)*
5. Divide the spinach, pepper, peas, onion, chilli and coriander between the 12 holes. *(no heat)*
6. In the jug whisk 8 eggs, 390 ml liquid egg whites (400 g), 1 tsp ground cumin (2 g), ¾ tsp red chilli powder (2 g), a heaped ¼ tsp haldi (turmeric powder, 1 g), ½ tsp black pepper (1 g) and a heaped ½ tsp salt (4 g) until no streaks of white are left. *(no heat · 2 min)*
7. Pour the egg over the vegetables until each hole is about ¾ full (they rise). *(no heat)*
8. Bake until puffed and set in the middle. *(oven 180 °C · 22 min)*
9. Check: push a knife into the middle of one muffin. It comes out clean, with no runny egg, and the tops are puffed and firm. Wet? 3–4 minutes more. *(no heat)*
10. Leave them 10 minutes in the tin, run a knife around each one and lift them onto the rack to cool while you make the roti. *(no heat · 10 min)*
11. Divide the dough into 4 equal balls of about 50 g (golf-ball size) and keep them under the cloth. Heat the tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan, with no oil, for 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
12. Roll one ball: dip it in dry atta, flatten it with your palm and roll it into a thin circle the size of a side plate (about 16 cm across, 2 mm thick). Give it a quarter turn every few rolls so it stays round, and dust with atta when it sticks. *(no heat · 1 min)*
13. First side: lay the roti on the hot dry pan. When small bubbles show (20–30 seconds), flip it. *(medium-high heat · 30 s)*
14. Second side: cook 30–40 seconds, pressing the edges gently with a folded cloth so it puffs. Flip once more for 10 seconds, then lay it on a clean tea towel. *(medium-high heat · 1 min)*
15. Roll and cook the other 3 the same way, rolling the next one while one cooks. On a first try each one takes 2–3 minutes, so allow about 8 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 8 min)*
16. Let the roti cool fully, spread out on the tea towel (about 10 minutes), before you bag them: bagged warm, they go soggy. *(no heat · 10 min)*
17. Pack 4 portions: 3 cooled muffins and 1 roti each, baking paper between the roti. *(no heat)*

**Done when:** Muffins: puffed and firm on top; a knife pushed into the middle comes out clean, with no runny egg. Roti: light brown spots on both sides, no pale or doughy patches, soft when folded.

**Box it up:** Cool and put away within 1 hour. Each portion is 3 muffins and 1 roti. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. To freeze, lay the muffins flat on a tray for 1 hour first, then bag them with their roti.

**Reheat:** Muffins from the fridge: microwave 30–40 seconds; from frozen: 60–90 seconds, turning once, until hot all through. Roti: 20–30 seconds a side on a hot dry pan (from frozen, 30–40 seconds a side), or 15 seconds in the microwave under a damp paper towel.

**Tips**

- Liquid egg whites are "claras de huevo pasteurizadas" in a carton at Mercadona, in the egg aisle.
- Muffins sticking to the tin? Use silicone moulds or paper cases next time rather than more oil.
- They sink a little as they cool. That is normal.
- Too spicy? Leave out the green chillies.
- Short on time? Buy fresh or frozen wholemeal roti (chapati) at the Pakistani grocery near the halal butcher and warm each on a dry pan 30 seconds a side. Shop roti are often bigger and some have oil in them, so the numbers in the app change a little.
- Atta is wholemeal chapati flour ("chakki atta"), sold in 5 kg bags at the Pakistani grocery. Mercadona's harina integral de trigo also works; the roti come out a little firmer.

## Changes made to meet the breakfast targets

- Half the whole eggs of a 12-muffin batch are swapped for liquid egg whites (as the ADA recipe does with 4 whites): 3 muffins then give about 27 g protein without going over 300 kcal.
- The references use cheese; it is left out (protein comes from egg white instead, and it avoids the animal-rennet question). Peas and a wholemeal roti lift fibre to the breakfast target of 5 g.
