## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Wide deep frying pan or karahi (28 cm) with a lid
- Wooden spoon
- Grater
- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop for rolling
- Tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan (26–28 cm)
- Clean tea towel
- Tray for cooling
- 4 flat freezer bags or boxes

**Before you start**

- Chop 1½ medium onions (160 g) fine (5 mm). Chop 2 medium tomatoes (240 g) small.
- Peel 5 garlic cloves (15 g) and a 4 cm piece of ginger (15 g); grate both into a paste.
- Chop 2 green chillies (10 g). Whisk 2 tbsp yogurt (30 g) smooth in a cup. Chop a small handful of coriander (8 g).

**Steps**

1. Roti dough first, so it rests while you cook: in a big bowl mix 235 g atta (wholemeal chapati flour, about 2 cups) with a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour in 165 ml water (a little under ¾ cup) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until it comes together in one soft, slightly sticky ball. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Knead 5 minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, repeat. Wet your hand if it sticks. It is ready when it is smooth and soft, like an earlobe. *(no heat · 5 min)*
3. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and leave the dough to rest while you cook (at least 20 minutes: a rested dough rolls thin without tearing). *(no heat)*
4. Heat 2 tbsp + 1 tsp sunflower oil (32 g) in the pan for 1 minute. *(medium heat · 1 min)*
5. Add the onion and stir every minute or two until golden brown (not dark). *(medium heat · 9 min)*
6. Add the ginger-garlic paste and stir 1 minute. *(medium heat · 1 min)*
7. Add the mince (520 g: the butcher weighs it). Break it up with the spoon and cook on high, stirring, until the water it lets out has dried off and no pink is left. *(high heat · 10 min)*
8. Add the tomatoes and the whisked yogurt, then 1 heaped tsp salt (7 g), 1½ tsp red chilli powder (4 g), 2 tsp ground coriander (4 g), 1 tsp ground cumin (2 g) and a heaped ½ tsp haldi (turmeric powder, 2 g). Stir, lid on, cook 10 minutes. *(low heat · 10 min)*
9. Add the frozen peas (320 g (about 2½ cups)) and the green chillies. Lid on, 8 minutes. *(medium heat · 8 min)*
10. Lid off. Stir until there is no watery juice left and the oil separates at the edges. *(medium-high heat · 4 min)*
11. Turn off the heat. Stir in 1 tsp garam masala (2 g) and the coriander. *(no heat)*
12. Check: push the keema aside. No liquid runs into the gap, no pink meat is left, oil glistens at the edges and the peas are soft. Wet? Stir on high 2 minutes more. *(no heat)*
13. Spread the keema on the tray to cool and set a timer for 30 minutes. When it rings, put the tray in the fridge, even if the roti are not finished: cooked food never sits out more than 1 hour. *(no heat)*
14. Divide the dough into 8 equal balls of about 50 g (golf-ball size) and keep them under the cloth. Heat the tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan, with no oil, for 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
15. Roll one ball: dip it in dry atta, flatten it with your palm and roll it into a thin circle the size of a side plate (about 16 cm across, 2 mm thick). Give it a quarter turn every few rolls so it stays round, and dust with atta when it sticks. *(no heat · 1 min)*
16. First side: lay the roti on the hot dry pan. When small bubbles show (20–30 seconds), flip it. *(medium-high heat · 30 s)*
17. Second side: cook 30–40 seconds, pressing the edges gently with a folded cloth so it puffs. Flip once more for 10 seconds, then lay it on a clean tea towel. *(medium-high heat · 1 min)*
18. Roll and cook the other 7 the same way, rolling the next one while one cooks. On a first try each one takes 2–3 minutes, so allow about 18 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 18 min)*
19. Let the roti cool fully, spread out on the tea towel (about 10 minutes), before you bag them: bagged warm, they go soggy. *(no heat · 10 min)*
20. Pack 4 portions: ¼ of the cold keema in a flat freezer bag or box, and 2 cooled roti in a small bag (baking paper between them). *(no heat)*

**Done when:** Keema: no pink meat, no loose liquid when you push it aside, oil glistening at the edges, peas soft. Roti: light brown spots on both sides, no pale or doughy patches, soft when folded.

**Box it up:** Cool on a wide tray and put away within 1 hour. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. Each portion goes with its 2 roti.

**Reheat:** Move a frozen portion to the fridge the night before. Keema: microwave covered 2½–3 minutes, stirring halfway, until steaming hot all through (from frozen: defrost setting first, then 4–5 minutes), or in a pan with a splash of water. Reheat once only. Roti: 20–30 seconds a side on a hot dry pan (from frozen, 30–40 seconds a side), or 15 seconds in the microwave under a damp paper towel.

**Tips**

- Halal: buy the beef from the halal butcher (carnicería halal). Check the yogurt label says only milk and cultures (no gelatine).
- Ask the butcher for "carne picada magra de ternera", about 10 % fat.
- Ginger-garlic paste: peel the garlic and ginger and grate both on the smallest holes of a grater, or blend them with 1 tsp water. A jar of ready paste from the Pakistani grocery is fine (use the same grams).
- Too spicy? Leave out the green chillies.
- If it catches on the bottom, add 2 tbsp water and scrape.
- Short on time? Buy fresh or frozen wholemeal roti (chapati) at the Pakistani grocery near the halal butcher and warm each on a dry pan 30 seconds a side. Shop roti are often bigger and some have oil in them, so the numbers in the app change a little.
