## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Food processor or chopper (or a grater and a sharp knife)
- Clean tea towel for squeezing
- Big mixing bowl
- Oven tray lined with baking paper (skewers optional)
- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop for rolling
- Tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan (26–28 cm)
- Clean tea towel
- Wire rack
- 4 freezer bags, 1 lunch box and 1 small lidded pot for each day

**Before you start**

- Peel ½ medium onion (80 g), 4 garlic cloves (12 g) and a 3 cm piece of ginger (10 g).

**Steps**

1. Roti dough first, so it rests while you cook: in a big bowl mix 235 g atta (wholemeal chapati flour, about 2 cups) with a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour in 165 ml water (a little under ¾ cup) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until it comes together in one soft, slightly sticky ball. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Knead 5 minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, repeat. Wet your hand if it sticks. It is ready when it is smooth and soft, like an earlobe. *(no heat · 5 min)*
3. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and leave the dough to rest while you cook (at least 20 minutes: a rested dough rolls thin without tearing). *(no heat)*
4. Blitz the onion, 2–3 green chillies (12 g), a handful of coriander (12 g), a few mint leaves (6 g), the garlic and the ginger until finely chopped (or grate the onion, garlic and ginger and chop the rest very fine). Squeeze the mix hard in the tea towel to get the onion juice out. *(no heat · 5 min)*
5. In the bowl add the mince (640 g), 1½ tsp roasted ground cumin (3 g), 2 tsp crushed coriander seeds (4 g), 1 tsp red chilli powder (3 g), 1½ tsp garam masala (3 g) and 1½ tsp salt (9 g). Knead with your hand 3–4 minutes until sticky. *(no heat · 4 min)*
6. Cover and chill 30 minutes so the kebabs hold together. *(no heat · 30 min)*
7. Heat the oven to 220 °C. Brush the lined tray with the oil (16 g, about 1 tbsp + 1 tsp). *(oven 220 °C · 10 min)*
8. With wet hands shape 16 long kebabs of about 50 g (12 cm long), on skewers or straight on the tray. Roll them in the oil on the tray. *(no heat · 8 min)*
9. Bake 9 minutes, turn them, then 6–9 minutes more until browned. *(oven 220 °C · 17 min)*
10. Check: break the thickest kebab open. Browned outside, no pink inside, juices clear. Pink? 3 minutes more in the oven. *(no heat)*
11. Spread the kebabs on the tray to cool and set a timer for 30 minutes. When it rings, put the tray in the fridge, even if the roti are not finished: cooked food never sits out more than 1 hour. *(no heat)*
12. Divide the dough into 8 equal balls of about 50 g (golf-ball size) and keep them under the cloth. Heat the tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan, with no oil, for 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
13. Roll one ball: dip it in dry atta, flatten it with your palm and roll it into a thin circle the size of a side plate (about 16 cm across, 2 mm thick). Give it a quarter turn every few rolls so it stays round, and dust with atta when it sticks. *(no heat · 1 min)*
14. First side: lay the roti on the hot dry pan. When small bubbles show (20–30 seconds), flip it. *(medium-high heat · 30 s)*
15. Second side: cook 30–40 seconds, pressing the edges gently with a folded cloth so it puffs. Flip once more for 10 seconds, then lay it on a clean tea towel. *(medium-high heat · 1 min)*
16. Roll and cook the other 7 the same way, rolling the next one while one cooks. On a first try each one takes 2–3 minutes, so allow about 18 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 18 min)*
17. Let the roti cool fully, spread out on the tea towel (about 10 minutes), before you bag them: bagged warm, they go soggy. *(no heat · 10 min)*
18. Pack 4 cold kebabs and 2 cooled roti per bag. *(no heat)*
19. Kachumber and raita (no cooking), on the day or the night before, for 1 portion: dice 80 g tomato, 80 g cucumber and 30 g red onion (1 cm); toss with 1½ tsp lemon juice (7 g), a pinch of roasted cumin and a pinch of salt. Raita: 60 g yogurt (4 tbsp) whisked with a few chopped mint leaves, a pinch of cumin and a pinch of salt, in its own pot. *(no heat · 5 min)*

**Done when:** Break the thickest kebab open: browned outside, no pink inside, juices clear (75 °C if you have a thermometer). Roti: light brown spots on both sides, no pale or doughy patches, soft when folded.

**Box it up:** Cool and put away within 1 hour. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. The roti set the limits: kebabs alone keep 3 months frozen. Kachumber and raita keep 2 days and cannot be frozen, so mix them fresh for each dinner (5 minutes).

**Reheat:** Kebabs from frozen: oven or air fryer at 200 °C for 10–12 minutes, or microwave 2 minutes then 2 minutes in a hot pan, until hot all through. Roti: 20–30 seconds a side on a hot dry pan (from frozen, 30–40 seconds a side), or 15 seconds in the microwave under a damp paper towel. Kachumber cold. Never microwave the raita or yogurt pot: it splits and goes watery. Eat it cold.

**Tips**

- Halal: buy the beef from the halal butcher (carnicería halal). Check the yogurt label says only milk and cultures (no gelatine).
- Ask the butcher for "carne picada magra de ternera", about 10 % fat. Lean mince needs the 30 minutes in the fridge to hold its shape.
- Kebabs falling off the skewers? Squeeze the onion harder and shape them flat on the tray instead.
- Too spicy? Use 1 green chilli instead of 2–3.
- Roasted ground cumin (bhuna zeera): toast 1 tbsp cumin seeds in a dry pan on medium heat for about 1 minute, until they smell nutty and darken one shade, then crush them with a rolling pin or in a mortar. Keep it in a jar for all the raitas and chaats.
- Short on time? Buy fresh or frozen wholemeal roti (chapati) at the Pakistani grocery near the halal butcher and warm each on a dry pan 30 seconds a side. Shop roti are often bigger and some have oil in them, so the numbers in the app change a little.
