## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop
- Your biggest dry non-stick frying pan (28 cm) or a large tawa
- Clean tea towel
- Grater
- Bowl and fork for the eggs
- Tray for cooling
- Baking paper, foil and a freezer bag

**Before you start**

- Cut the chicken breast (280 g) into thin strips (1 cm).
- Cut 1 red pepper (120 g) into strips. Slice ¾ medium red onion (80 g) thin.
- Peel and grate 2 garlic cloves (6 g) and a 2 cm piece of ginger (6 g) into a paste.

**Steps**

1. Roti dough first, so it rests while you cook: in a big bowl mix 176 g atta (wholemeal chapati flour, about 1½ cups) with a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour in 123 ml water (about ½ cup) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until it comes together in one soft, slightly sticky ball. *(no heat · 3 min)*
2. Knead 5 minutes: push the dough away with the heel of your hand, fold it back, turn it, repeat. Wet your hand if it sticks. It is ready when it is smooth and soft, like an earlobe. *(no heat · 5 min)*
3. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and leave the dough to rest while you cook (at least 20 minutes: a rested dough rolls thin without tearing). *(no heat)*
4. Chicken: in the frying pan, add a little under 1 tbsp oil (12 g) and heat 1 minute. *(high heat · 1 min)*
5. Add the chicken strips and stir-fry until white all through. *(high heat · 5 min)*
6. Add the pepper, onion, ginger-garlic paste and the powders: 1 tsp ground cumin (2 g), 1¼ tsp paprika (3 g), ¾ tsp red chilli powder (2 g) and ½ tsp salt (3 g). Stir-fry 3 minutes until the pepper softens. Turn off the heat and squeeze in 2 tsp lemon juice (10 g). *(medium-high heat · 3 min)*
7. Egg: whisk 4 eggs with 120 g liquid egg whites and a pinch of salt (1 g). Pour into the same pan (no extra oil) and stir slowly with the spatula until just set: soft, but no runny egg. *(medium heat · 2 min)*
8. Check: cut the thickest chicken strip. White all through, no pink. The egg is set, with no runny parts. Pink? 2 more minutes in the pan. *(no heat)*
9. Spread the egg on the tray next to the chicken and let both cool while you make the roti (hot filling makes the wrap soggy). *(no heat)*
10. Divide the dough into 4 equal balls of about 75 g (golf-ball size or a little bigger) and keep them under the cloth. Heat your biggest frying pan or tawa, with no oil, for 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
11. Roll one ball: dip it in dry atta, flatten it with your palm and roll it into a thin circle as big as your pan (about 25 cm across, 2 mm thick). Give it a quarter turn every few rolls so it stays round, and dust with atta when it sticks. *(no heat · 1 min)*
12. First side: lay the roti on the hot dry pan. When small bubbles show (20–30 seconds), flip it. *(medium-high heat · 30 s)*
13. Second side: cook 30–40 seconds, pressing the edges gently with a folded cloth so it puffs. Flip once more for 10 seconds, then lay it on a clean tea towel. *(medium-high heat · 1 min)*
14. Roll and cook the other 3 the same way, rolling the next one while one cooks. On a first try each one takes 2–3 minutes, so allow about 8 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 8 min)*
15. Let the roti cool fully, spread out on the tea towel (about 10 minutes), before you bag them: bagged warm, they go soggy. *(no heat · 10 min)*
16. Lay a roti flat. Put ¼ of the chicken and ¼ of the egg in a line across the lower third. Fold the sides in, then roll it up tightly from the bottom. Wrap in baking paper, then foil. Make all 4. *(no heat · 5 min)*

**Done when:** Chicken: cut the thickest strip, white all through, no pink. Egg: set, with no runny parts. Roti: light brown spots on both sides, no pale or doughy patches, soft when folded.

**Box it up:** Cool and put away within 1 hour. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it.

**Reheat:** From the fridge: dry pan 2 minutes a side, or microwave 60–75 seconds. From frozen: take off the foil (never microwave foil), wrap in a damp paper towel and microwave 2 minutes, turning once, until hot all through.

**Tips**

- Halal: chicken from the halal butcher. The big home roti replaces shop wraps, which list E471 without saying it is from plants.
- A 25 cm roti is hard to roll at first. If it tears, make 2 smaller roti per wrap from the same dough instead.
- Too spicy? Use ¼ tsp chilli powder.
- Ginger-garlic paste: peel the garlic and ginger and grate both on the smallest holes of a grater, or blend them with 1 tsp water. A jar of ready paste from the Pakistani grocery is fine (use the same grams).
- Atta is wholemeal chapati flour ("chakki atta"), sold in 5 kg bags at the Pakistani grocery. Mercadona's harina integral de trigo also works; the roti come out a little firmer.
