## Method

This is the method the app shows under "How to make it" (generated from the same data).

**Equipment**

- Pot (2 l) for the potatoes
- Potato masher or fork
- Big mixing bowl
- Rolling pin (belan) and a clean worktop
- Tawa or a dry non-stick frying pan (26–28 cm)
- Flat spatula and a pastry brush (or a teaspoon)
- Wire rack
- Grater
- Baking paper, a freezer bag and 4 small lidded pots (about 250 ml)

**Before you start**

- Chop ¼ red onion very fine (40 g), 1–2 green chillies (8 g) and a small handful of coriander (8 g).
- Measure the oil for frying into a small cup: 24 g (about 1¾ tbsp), 6 g for each paratha.

**Steps**

1. Put 2½ medium potatoes (520 g) in their skins in the pot, cover with cold water and bring to the boil. *(high heat · 5 min)*
2. Simmer until a knife slides in easily. *(medium heat · 20 min)*
3. Drain and let them cool until you can hold them. Peel and mash them smooth: lumps tear the paratha. Let the mash cool completely. *(no heat · 20 min)*
4. Filling: mix the mash with the onion, chilli, coriander, 1 tsp roasted crushed cumin (2 g), ¾ tsp red chilli powder (2 g) and ½ tsp salt (3 g). Shape into 4 balls. *(no heat · 3 min)*
5. Dough: in the bowl mix the atta (200 g, about 1⅔ cups) with ½ tsp salt (3 g). Add the water (140 ml) a little at a time, mixing with your fingers, until you have a soft dough that only just sticks; you may not need every drop. *(no heat · 3 min)*
6. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. *(no heat · 5 min)*
7. Cover with a damp cloth and let the dough rest 20 minutes. *(no heat · 20 min)*
8. Divide the dough into 4 balls (about 85 g each). Dust one with atta and roll it to a 10 cm circle. *(no heat)*
9. Put a filling ball in the middle. Bring the edges up around it like a pouch, pinch them together and twist off any extra dough. Press it flat with your palm, sealed side down. *(no heat)*
10. Dust well with atta and roll gently from the centre out to 18–20 cm. If filling pokes through, pat dry atta on the spot and carry on. *(no heat)*
11. Heat the dry tawa or pan 2 minutes. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
12. Cook the paratha dry 1 minute on each side, until it has pale spots. *(medium-high heat · 2 min)*
13. Brush the top with ¾ tsp oil (3 g), flip, and press it down with the spatula for 1 minute. Brush the other side with ¾ tsp oil (3 g), flip, and cook 1 minute more until both sides have golden-brown spots. *(medium heat · 2 min)*
14. Check this first paratha: golden-brown spots on both sides, crisp edges and no white, raw-looking dough. Pale? 30 seconds more a side, and cook the next ones a little longer. *(no heat)*
15. Fill, roll and cook the other 3 parathas the same way (about 4 minutes each on the pan; roll the next one while one cooks). *(medium-high heat · 12 min)*
16. Cool the parathas on the rack, not in a stack (stacked hot, they go soggy). *(no heat · 20 min)*
17. Raita pots (no cooking), 2 now: grate 80 g cucumber and squeeze it hard. Whisk 400 g protein yogurt smooth and stir in the cucumber, half the chopped mint (3 g), ½ tsp roasted ground cumin (1 g) and a pinch of salt (1 g). Split into 2 pots (200 g of yogurt each). Mix the other 2 on Tuesday night. *(no heat · 3 min)*
18. When they are cool, stack the parathas with baking paper between them and bag them as Box it up says. *(no heat)*

**Done when:** Golden-brown spots on both sides, crisp edges and no white, raw-looking dough anywhere.

**Box it up:** Cool and put away within 1 hour, parathas stacked with baking paper between them. Portions 1–2 in the fridge: eat them within 2 days (by Tuesday if you make them on Sunday). Portions 3–4 in the freezer: eat within 2 months, and move one to the fridge the night before you eat it. Raita cannot be frozen and keeps 2 days: 2 pots on Sunday, 2 on Tuesday night (or mix one the night before, 2 minutes).

**Reheat:** Paratha from frozen: straight onto a dry pan on medium heat, 2–3 minutes a side, until hot and crisp. Or microwave 1 minute, then 1 minute on the pan. Never microwave the raita or yogurt pot: it splits and goes watery. Eat it cold.

**Tips**

- Paratha tearing when you roll? The filling was still warm or lumpy, or the dough did not rest. Cool the mash fully and roll gently from the middle out.
- Weigh the frying oil: it is most of the calories, and the app counts 6 g per paratha.
- Too spicy? Leave out the green chilli and the chilli powder in the filling.
- Protein yogurt is the high-protein plain yogurt on the shopping list (milk and cultures only, no gelatine).
- Atta is wholemeal chapati flour ("chakki atta"), sold in 5 kg bags at the Pakistani grocery. Mercadona's harina integral de trigo also works; the roti come out a little firmer.
- Roasted ground cumin (bhuna zeera): toast 1 tbsp cumin seeds in a dry pan on medium heat for about 1 minute, until they smell nutty and darken one shade, then crush them with a rolling pin or in a mortar. Keep it in a jar for all the raitas and chaats.

## Changes made to meet the breakfast targets

- Fat cut from Food Fusion's 1 tbsp ghee (12.8 g) + 3.6 g filling oil per paratha to 6 g oil on the tawa: the paratha alone drops from about 415 to about 330 kcal.
- A paratha alone has about 8 g protein, so the portion comes with a 200 g protein-yogurt raita (20 g protein) to reach the 25 g breakfast target.
- Yield: Zainab's measured-moisture yield (0.651) is applied to the whole raw paratha, oil included; with less fat than her version this slightly overstates the cooked weight (about 2%).
- Dough water 140 g for 200 g atta (0.7, the roti standard) instead of 1:1 (Zainab, 2026-10-16: 1:1 is too wet to roll a stuffed paratha). The cooked weight is set by the target moisture, so the yield factor becomes 0.692 of the smaller raw total and the portion numbers do not change.
