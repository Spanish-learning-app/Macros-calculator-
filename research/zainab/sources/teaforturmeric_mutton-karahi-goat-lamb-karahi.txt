# Easy Mutton Karahi (Goat/Lamb Karahi) - Restaurant-Style!
https://www.teaforturmeric.com/mutton-karahi-goat-lamb-karahi/
yield: ["4","4 servings"]
- 1.5 lb bone-in goat or lamb meat
- 1.5 cups water
- 1/2 tbsp crushed garlic
- 1/2 tbsp crushed ginger
- 1/2 small yellow onion (chopped)
- 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/3-1/2 cup neutral oil
- 2 medium Serrano or Indian finger chili peppers (halved lengthwise or left whole for less heat)
- 4-5  garlic cloves (finely chopped or crushed using a mortar and pestle)
- 1/2- inch ginger (finely chopped or crushed using a mortar and pestle)
- 5  ripe medium Roma or Vine tomatoes (cored and halved lengthwise – ensure they’re ripe! - Note 2)
- 1 1/2 tsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp cumin powder (or cumin seeds)
- 1 tsp Kashmiri red chili powder
- 1/2 tsp red chili flakes
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 3 tbsp plain whole milk yogurt (whisked)
- 1-2 tbsp salted butter
- 1 tsp black peppercorns (freshly ground using a mortar and pestle, plus more for garnish)
- 1/2 tsp lemon juice
- 3-4 small green chili peppers (Thai/bird&#39;s eye) (slit in half)
- 1 tbsp julienned ginger
- 2 tbsp cilantro (finely chopped)
1. Place a large, heavy-bottomed pan over high heat (this pot will be used to boil the meat and cook the Karahi). Add all of the ingredients listed under ‘To Cook the Meat’ and stir to mix. Submerge the meat so the water covers as much of it as possible. Bring to a light boil, then reduce the heat to a simmer (low-medium). Cover and cook for at least 1 hour and 15 minutes (See Note 3), stirring mid-way, until the meat is fully cooked but not fall-off-the-bone tender. The meat should break when pressed with a wooden spoon, but it shouldn’t be super tender as it’ll finish cooking later. If it’s not breaking when pressed, cover and allow to cook for another 10-15 minutes. There will still be some water left with the meat.
2. Increase the heat to high. Sauté for 7-8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until most of the water has evaporated and you can start to see the fat from the meat. To prevent overcooking the meat, it helps to shove the meat to one side while allowing the water to boil. Add the oil and green chili pepper and sauté until the meat sears lightly, 2-3 minutes. Add the garlic and ginger and sauté for another 1 minute.
3. Add ¼ cup water and the halved tomatoes (Note 3) and stir to mix. Reduce the heat to medium. Cover and simmer for 6-7 minutes. Uncover and use a tong to remove the skins of the tomatoes as they become easy to remove. How long you’ll need to steam them depends on how ripe the tomatoes are. At this stage, the tomatoes will start to release their own water.
4. Increase the heat to high and sauté for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally. Use a wooden spoon to gently mash the tomatoes to help it meld into the masala. Don’t worry if all the tomatoes don’t quite mash just yet.
5. Add the coriander powder, cumin powder, Kashmiri chili, red chili flakes, and salt. Continue to sauté for another 5 minutes, using a wooden spoon to gently mash the tomatoes so they disintegrate and meld with the curry. (Bhunai is super important here. As the restaurant chefs say, the longer the bhunai, the better the flavor.) Taste and add salt, if needed.
6. If the karahi reduces too much while the tomatoes aren’t fully disintegrated, add ½ cup water and continue to mash the tomatoes to help them break down. You’ll notice the Karahi taking on a glossy appearance as the oil starts to separate and line the sides of the pan. The meat should be tender and should break easily when pressed with a wooden spoon.
7. Add the whisked yogurt. Gently sauté for 2-3 minutes, until it melds into the Karahi and the oil separates again.
8. Reduce the heat to low. Add butter, freshly ground black peppercorns, and lemon juice. Stir gently to combine. Garnish with green chili peppers, julienned ginger, cilantro, and more freshly ground black pepper, if you’d like. Turn off the heat and serve with naan, roti, or other bread.
