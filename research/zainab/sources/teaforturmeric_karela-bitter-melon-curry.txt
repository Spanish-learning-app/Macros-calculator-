# Karela (Bitter Melon Curry) - No Bitterness!
https://www.teaforturmeric.com/karela-bitter-melon-curry/
yield: ["4"]
- 1 pound Indian variety bitter melon/gourd (karela)
- 2 + 3/4 tsp kosher salt (divided)
- 1/4 cup + 2 tbsp  neutral oil (divided, more as needed)
- 2  medium yellow onions (quartered and thinly sliced)
- 5  garlic cloves (minced or crushed)
- 1 inch fresh ginger (minced or crushed)
- 2-3  Roma tomatoes (diced or finely chopped)
- 1-2  Serrano peppers or Indian finger chili (halved lengthwise or roughly chopped for more heat)
- 1 tbsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 3/4 tsp amchur powder (dry mango powder) (OR 1 tbsp pomegranate powder (anardana seeds ground to a powder))
- 1/2 tsp red chili powder
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1-2 tbsp plain whole-milk yogurt (whisked, optional)
- 2 tbsp store-bought or homemade crispy fried onions (optional)
- 1-2 tbsp finely chopped cilantro (for garnish)
1. Use a peeler to lightly peel the rough outer skin to remove the bumps and make the exterior mostly smooth. (This can get messy! My mother in law usually peels them outside.) Trim the ends, halve lengthwise, then scoop out the thick white pith and seeds with a spoon. (For larger melons, halve lengthwise again, so the pieces are about ¾ to 1” long, then thinly slice widthwise into 1/4-1/2” thick pieces. You want slices that will remain distinct in your final masala, but not too thick that they are tough.)
2. Place in a medium bowl, sprinkle with 2 tsp kosher salt, then massage the salt into the bitter melon. Cover and let rest for 2–3 hours, or up to overnight in the fridge.
3. Place the karele in a strainer or colander, rinse, then blot dry with a paper towel to squeeze out excess moisture and extract the bitter juices. (You&#39;ll have around ~300g after they&#39;re chopped/cleaned/blotted, depending on how much was peeled.)
4. Heat ¼ cup oil in a large, wide pan over high heat. Add the bitter gourd and sauté for 13-14 minutes, until it goes from bright green to deep green, with golden-brown edges. It should be lightly crisp, but still juicy, without getting too dark or brittle. Remove with a slotted spoon onto a paper towel-lined plate and set aside. (Clean off the pan, if needed.)
5. Reduce heat to medium-high. Add the remaining 2 tbsp oil and onions. Sauté 10-12 minutes, until lightly golden and browning at the edges - you don’t want them too deeply golden brown. If you find there isn’t enough oil for the onions to saute, add another 2-3 tbsp. Add the garlic and ginger and saute for 2 minutes, until the onions deepen even more in color. Deglaze with 3 tbsp water to soften and help even out the color of the onions. Add the tomatoes, green chilies, amchur/ground pomegranate, coriander powder, red chili powder, cumin seeds, turmeric, and remaining 3/4 tsp salt. Continue to cook for 2-3 minutes, until the tomatoes soften and release water. Stir in the yogurt.
6. Return the bitter gourd to the pan and stir to coat in the masala. Reduce heat to low, cover, and cook for 20 minutes, stirring halfway through. As with most veggies, you want them to cook in their own juices for best flavor. If it’s sticking to the bottom, add a 2-3 tbsp water. At this stage, the bitter gourd should be very tender, breaking easily when pressed with  a wooden spoon. Stir in the crispy fried onions (if using), then cover and steam for another 2-3 minutes. Turn off the heat and garnish with cilantro. Serve hot with roti, naan, or paratha.
