# Nihari
https://www.flourandspiceblog.com/nihari/
yield: undefined
- 2  lb  Large chunks of beef/veal - 3 inch pieces
- 1-2 lb bones
- 2 tbsp salt
- 1 1/2 tbsp ginger paste
- 1 1/2 tbsp garlic paste
- 1 tbsp  red chilli powder
- 1 tbsp  kashmiri chilli powder
- 1 tbsp coriander powder
- 1/2 tsp turmeric powder
- Ghee/Oil for cooking
- 1 large onion
- 1.5 tbsp  coriander seeds
- 1.5 tbsp  saunf
- 1.5 tsp whole black pepper
- 1.5 tsp whole cloves
- 1/2 tbsp cumin seeds
- 1  bay leaf
- 1/2 tsp grated nutmeg
- 2 1-inch  cinnamon sticks
- 2-3 piece mace
- 3  black cardamoms
- 4-6  green cardamoms
- 1 piece dried ginger (small, can sub with 1/2 tbsp of gingepowder)
- 6  whole red chillies (round)
- 1 tsp kalonji
- 2  pieces of peepli or 3-4 kabab chini (optional)
- lemon wedges
- sliced green chillies
- julienned ginger
1. Dry roast all the ingredients in a frying pan (no oil), grind to a powder. Set aside 
2. Heat oil in a large pot
3. Thinly slice your onion, fry till golden brown then spread on paper towels to dry. (we will use these at the end)
4. Add all the remaining ingredients in the first list  and stir fry the meat until its browned and the masala is cooked - about 4-5 minutes
5. Then add your Nihari Masala Mix and 7 cups of water and stir well.
6. Pressure cook for 45-50 minutes or slow cook for 4-5 hours undisturbed to get beautifully tender meat. For slow cooking stove top, bring the mixture to a boil then let it simmer covered.
7. Once the time is up skim any greyish scum that may rise to the top and discard. 
8. Crush the onions and mix in, bring the nihari to a boil
9. If you are serving this another time then set it aside for the nihari to cool.
10. When ready to eat then dissolve 1/3 cup atta in 1 cup water and whisk briskly as you add it to the nihari
11. The nihari will thicken as it cooks - 10 to 15 minutes
12. Adjust consistency per liking (more water or atta+water), just remember you need to simmer after adding it to lose the raw taste.
13. Heat a few tbsp of oil in a small saucepan and add 1.5 tbsp of kashmiri laal mirch (for colour) and add it on top of the nihari like a tadka/baghaar.
